## Where the growth in spending went

Yearly General Fund spending rose by {{fact:spending_growth_fy11_25}} from FY2011 to FY2025. Every
dollar of that rise can be assigned to a cause:

{{chart:causes}}

**Inflation is the largest piece by far.** The same staff doing the same work cost more each year
as pay, prices and health premiums rose. Measured against national benchmarks for public
employers, Brookline's costs rose almost exactly as fast as everyone else's.

**Then buildings and staff.** Debt service rose from $9.5 million to $36.3 million a year, mostly
for school buildings voters approved separately. And the schools added staff faster than
students: 6.75 students for each school staff member in FY2011, 5.62 in FY2025.

**Growth added little.** New homes and commercial space account for about
{{fact:cause_m_new_homes_commercial_space}} of the rise, about {{fact:cause_pct_new_homes_commercial_space}}.

## Revenue: what kept pace, and what did not

The part of revenue Brookline controls, the tax levy with its own new growth and the fees it
sets, grew about as fast as costs. State aid grew about 2% a year and fell behind. Had it kept
pace, it would have been $9.5 million a year higher by 2025. That is 87% of the gap between costs and what revenue
would have raised on its own, without overrides: the gap the overrides filled.

## What this means for the forecast

The same causes drive the years ahead. How fast pay and health premiums grow decides the largest
piece, the school staffing ratio much of the rest, and new homes add relatively little cost.
[The decisions that matter](@decisions) puts a number on each.
