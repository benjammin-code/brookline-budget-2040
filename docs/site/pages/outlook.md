## The outlook

Starting from the budget Town Meeting adopted in May 2026, and assuming costs and revenue keep
behaving as they have since FY2011, revenue covers spending every year and runs ahead of it from FY2028. The gap, the
[[room]], is about {{fact:room_fy2028}} in FY2028, {{fact:room_2030s_low}} to
{{fact:room_2030s_high}} a year through most of the 2030s, and {{fact:room_fy2040}} in FY2040.
Without the override's revenue, the same years would run short.

{{chart:outlook}}

A projected gap is not a deficit anyone will run, since budgets must balance. The room measures
the size of the choices ahead, and Brookline has consistently spent close to what it has.

## What the base case assumes, and why

Each assumption continues what Brookline has done, or takes what the Town itself has set, rather
than betting on a change. The main ones:

- **Pay.** Town staff 3.0% a year, the target the Town bargains toward. School staff 3.7%, the
  middle of what the current contract's 2.75% raises plus step increases work out to by the
  School Department's own arithmetic. Police and fire 3.5%, close to the 3.3% a year their cost
  per position grew from FY2011 to FY2025, plus the fire steps and police positions already
  agreed.
- **Health premiums.** 12% a year in FY2028 and FY2029, then 8%. Recent increases have run 10%
  to 12%, and the 25-year average is about 6.5%; the base case settles between the two.
- **School staffing.** Staff fall about half as fast as enrollment, about ten positions a year,
  because that is what Brookline did from FY2019 to FY2025: enrollment fell 10.6% and staff
  5.0%. Retirements and departures would likely cover it. Children from new homes are staffed in
  full.
- **Enrollment.** Falling to {{fact:enroll_fy2040_about}} students by FY2040, following births.
  Children entering kindergarten through fall 2029 are already born, and resident births fell
  from about 590 a year in 2015 to 2019 to {{fact:births_ahead}} a year in 2022 to 2024. The
  forecast is the district consultant's, [[NESDEC]], scaled to the births actually counted.
- **Police and fire.** Staffing stays at the FY2027 budget, 128 sworn officers and 160
  firefighters. From FY2011 to FY2025 police and fire positions fell by five while building
  permits added {{fact:homes_permitted_fy11_25}} homes, so staffing has not tracked the number of
  homes. Fire staffing changes a whole company at a time, and the next one comes due at about
  {{fact:fire_company_homes}} homes, after FY2040 at the recent pace of building.
- **New growth.** The Town's budgeted $2.5 million in FY2027, then {{fact:trend_growth_rate}} of
  the levy a year, Brookline's rate over FY2022 to FY2026. That includes {{fact:homes_pace}} new
  homes a year, the pace of the past decade; the Chestnut Hill redevelopment is added on top.
- **Pensions and retiree health.** The pension reaches full funding in FY2030, on the Town's
  schedule. From FY2031 the money that frees up, $43.0 million a year with the current retiree
  health payment, pays down the retiree health liability, as the Town plans. Once that is paid
  off, in {{fact:opeb_paid_off}} on the actuary's trend, what to do with the money is a decision
  for then, so the base case sets it aside rather than counting it as room.

## Why the Town's own plan shows a deficit

The Town's long-range plan, presented in January 2026, shows a gap of $42.7 million in FY2031;
the base case shows {{fact:room_fy2031}} of room that year. The two answer different questions:
the Town's plan is deliberately cautious, while the base case asks what happens if costs and
revenue keep behaving as they have. Most of the difference is the override, which passed four
months after the plan was written, and the schools: the Town's plan grows school pay about 5% a
year and holds staff constant, while the base case grows pay about 3.7% and lets staff fall half
as fast as enrollment. The [Forecast Explorer](@explorer#vs) walks through each step between
the two.

## How sure this is

Run over FY2011 to FY2025 with what actually happened to staffing, enrollment and prices, the
forecast's method comes within about 4% of what Town departments spent and 6% for schools. It is
a continuation of the record, not a prediction, and the [Forecast Explorer](@explorer) lets anyone
change each assumption.
