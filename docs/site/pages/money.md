## The General Fund

The [[General Fund]] is the Town's main operating budget: schools, police and fire, public works,
libraries, employee benefits, debt and capital. Town Meeting adopted the FY2027 budget, with the
May 2026 [[override]], at $450.7 million.

{{chart:sankey}}

**Property tax** is $359.3 million, 80% of the total. Of that, $35.4 million is a
[[debt exclusion]]: tax voters approved to repay specific school and building loans, which
passes straight through to that debt. The rest, $323.9 million, is the ordinary levy, capped by
[[Proposition 2½]].

The other sources are [[local receipts]] such as motor vehicle excise, meals tax and fees
($37.6 million), [[state aid]] ($26.7 million), [[free cash]] ($18.3 million) and other available
funds ($8.8 million).

**Schools** are the largest use, $149.8 million. Town departments are $103.5 million and employee
benefits, mostly health insurance and pensions for current and retired staff, $99.1 million. The
rest pays for capital, reserves, debt and state assessments.

## The Town cannot simply collect more

When a home's [[assessed value]] goes up, the Town does not get more money. Under
[[Proposition 2½]], the total Brookline may collect from all property owners combined, the
[[levy limit]], rises about 2.5% a year whatever happens to house prices. Rising values change
how that total is divided among owners, not the total.

The Town can collect more than that in only two ways:

- **[[New growth]].** When something is built, renovated or converted, the value it adds is
  taxed on top of the 2.5%, permanently, with no vote.
- **An [[override]].** Voters raise the limit at the ballot. It is permanent, and it compounds,
  because every later year's 2.5% is calculated on the larger base.

A [[debt exclusion]] looks similar but pays for one specific building, most recently new
schools, and ends when that debt is paid off. It runs through the General Fund, but the tax
matches the debt payments dollar for dollar and pays for nothing else, so the forecast sets both
aside: leaving them in would add the same amount to revenue and spending and change nothing about
the balance between them.

## All five funds

Four smaller funds sit beside the General Fund. Water and sewer, the golf course and recreation
programs are paid for by the people who use them; community preservation is paid for by a 1%
property tax surcharge and a state match, and can be spent only on open space, historic
preservation, affordable housing and recreation.

{{chart:funds-sankey}}

The picture is simple once it is drawn: the General Fund is about 90% of the Town's money, and
the smaller funds mostly keep to themselves. The main thing that crosses between them is about
$4.0 million the enterprise funds pay the General Fund for their staff's benefits.
