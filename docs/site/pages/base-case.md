## The base case outlook

Starting from the budget Town Meeting adopted in May 2026 and continuing Brookline's own record
from FY2011 to FY2025, revenue covers spending every year and runs ahead of it from FY2028. The gap, the [[room]], is about
{{fact:room_fy2028}} in FY2028, {{fact:room_2030s_low}} to {{fact:room_2030s_high}} a year from
FY2031 through FY2038, and {{fact:room_fy2040}} in FY2040.

{{chart:outlook}}

The room peaks in FY2036 to FY2038 and then narrows. Health insurance keeps compounding at 8% a
year, so its yearly increase doubles from about $5 million in FY2031 to about $10 million by
FY2040, while revenue grows by a steadier amount each year. And as enrollment levels off near
FY2039, school staff fall more slowly, so the schools' budget grows faster. Budgets must balance every year by law, so a projected gap is not a
deficit anyone will run. It measures the size of the choices ahead. Brookline has consistently
spent close to what it has, so the room is best read as capacity for the decisions ahead to use.

Two parts of this outlook are less settled than the rest. The room depends on school staffing
continuing to adjust as enrollment falls: held at the FY2027 level instead, school staff would
cost {{fact:fy40_staff_keep_abs}} a year more by FY2040 and the room would
fall a little short in FY2028 and run out for good from {{fact:runs_out_staff_keep}}. And the near years are thin. Building permits in 2024 and 2025 were
the lowest since at least 2018. The base case already holds FY2027's new growth at the Town's
budgeted $2.5 million, about what they point to, but they also point to FY2028 new growth well
below the trend. If it comes in that low, FY2028 would have {{fact:fy2028_after_permits}} instead
of {{fact:room_fy2028}} of room, and each later year less, growing to {{fact:pl_effect_fy2040}} by FY2040. The state
certifies FY2027's new growth this fall.

## What the base case assumes

The base case continues Brookline's record rather than predicting a change in it. Its main
assumptions:

- **Pay.** Town staff 3.0% a year, the Town's bargaining target; schools 3.7%, what the current
  contract's 2.75% raises plus step increases work out to by the School Department's own
  arithmetic; Public Safety 3.5%, with the fire steps agreed for FY2028 and the four police
  positions to be refilled in FY2028 and FY2029 counted on top.
- **Health premiums.** 8% a year from FY2030. Retirees are about half of the health plan's
  members but, since many are on Medicare supplement plans, about 36% of its cost; their number
  grows about 0.9% a year, as it has since FY2011.
- **School staffing.** Falling about half as fast as enrollment, as from FY2019 to FY2025: about
  ten positions a year, while children from new homes are staffed in full. The School
  Department's own plan holds staff at the FY2027 level.
- **Police and fire.** Staffing at the FY2027 budget, 128 sworn police officers and 160
  firefighters. A fire company of 20 firefighters comes due once the town reaches about
  {{fact:fire_company_homes}} homes, which at the recent pace of building is after FY2040.
  Police cost grows a little with each new home. Other Town departments stay at their FY2027 staffing.
- **Enrollment.** The forecast by the district's consultant, [[NESDEC]], updated for the births
  since counted (2022 to 2024 averaged {{fact:births_ahead}} a year; NESDEC had estimated about 500),
  falling to {{fact:enroll_fy2040_about}} by FY2040. The School Department plans with a different
  forecast that expects a rebound; [Enrollment: three forecasts compared](@topic-enrollment) sets
  the three side by side.
- **New growth.** The Town's budgeted $2.5 million in FY2027, then {{fact:trend_growth_rate}} of
  the levy a year, Brookline's rate over FY2022 to FY2026, with the Chestnut Hill development
  added on top.
- **Pensions.** Full funding in FY2030; from FY2031 the roughly $37 million a year that frees up, together with the
  current retiree health payment, $43.0 million a year in all, pays down the retiree health ([[OPEB]]) liability, as the Town plans, until it is paid off. On
  the actuary's own trend for retiree health costs, that is in {{fact:opeb_paid_off}}, and the money
  is free again. What to do with it is a decision for then, so the base case sets it aside and
  doesn't count it as room.
- **State aid.** 2.5% a year from the FY2027 budget, the rate in the Town's own plan.
- **Prices.** 2.5% a year.
- **Capital and reserves.** The Select Board's policy from FY2028: 6.6% of the prior year's net
  revenue to capital, and the reserve fund and other non-departmental lines as Town policy sets
  them. What is left of [[free cash]] after those uses goes to capital and one-time spending, as
  the Town's free cash policy says, not to the operating budget.
- **Votes.** The May 2026 override phases in over FY2027 to FY2029; no further overrides or debt
  exclusions.

Put together, the base case adds almost no staff: Town departments hold at their FY2027 level,
and school staff fall more slowly than enrollment.

## Why the Town's own plan shows a deficit

The Town's long-range plan, presented in January 2026, shows a gap of $42.7 million in FY2031.
The base case shows {{fact:room_fy2031}} of room that year. The two answer different questions:
the Town's plan is deliberately cautious, because being wrong in the optimistic direction means
painful mid-year cuts, while the base case asks what happens if costs and revenue keep behaving
as they have.

Two differences explain most of the {{fact:walk_gap}} between them. **The override** passed in May
2026, four months after the Town's plan was written, and is worth $27.3 million a year by FY2031.
**School pay**: the Town's plan takes its school figures from the School Department's forecast,
made before any look at efficiencies, with salaries rising about 5% a year; the base case uses
about 3.7%, the middle of the range the School Department's own budget arithmetic gives for the
current contract, enrollment follows the decline in births, and staff fall half as fast as
enrollment where the Town's plan holds them constant. That step is worth
{{fact:walk_schools_abs}}.

## How sure this is

These are continuations of Brookline's own record, not predictions. As a check, the forecast's
method was run over FY2011 to FY2025, given what actually happened to staffing, enrollment and
prices: it came within about 4% of what Town departments actually spent in FY2025, and within
6% for schools.
