## Permits, the earliest reading for revenue

Work permitted in one year is mostly built over the next, valued on the January 1 after that, and
taxed as new growth in the fiscal year numbered three higher: 2024's permits feed FY2027. So
permits show new growth about two years ahead. Permits on taxable property came to
{{fact:pl_taxable_2024}} in 2024 and {{fact:pl_taxable_2025}} in 2025, against
{{fact:pl_taxable_fit_range}} a year from 2018 to 2023.

{{chart:gl-revenue}}

At past rates those permits point to real estate new growth of {{fact:pl_2027_expected}} in
FY2027 and {{fact:pl_2028_expected}} in FY2028, against {{fact:pl_2027_base}} and
{{fact:pl_2028_base}} in the base case. FY2027 is about what the base case already holds, the
Town's budgeted figure; FY2028 is well short of it. If new growth comes in at that level for two years and
then returns to its trend, the room would be {{fact:pl_effect_fy2028}} smaller in FY2028, leaving
{{fact:fy2028_after_permits}} instead of {{fact:room_fy2028}} of room, and {{fact:pl_effect_fy2040}}
smaller by FY2040. FY2028 is already the thinnest year in the base case, so this is the reading
most likely to change the near-term picture. The first check comes this fall, when the state
certifies the FY2027 new growth. Permits so far this year are on pace for
{{fact:pl_partial_annualized}}, closer to the earlier years.

These permits include buildings approved under Chapter 40B, the state's comprehensive permit
law, which take out building permits like any other construction. They do not include 40B homes
that are approved but not started. The Town's 2024 Housing Plan counted 352 of those, and none had
a large building permit by September 2026. If they start, permits would rise, and new growth
would follow about two years later.

## Births, the earliest reading for the schools

A year's births set the kindergarten class about five years later, and that class's size through
grade 12. Brookline residents had {{fact:ind_births}} babies in {{fact:ind_births_year}}, close to
the {{fact:births_ahead}} a year the base case assumes (the 2022 to 2024 average), and well below the
{{fact:ind_births_nesdec}} NESDEC assumed and the {{fact:ind_births_cm}} in the School Department's
forecast. If births stay near that level, enrollment keeps falling and the schools keep open
seats. Each new year's count is a test of the base case's assumption. The base case already
assumes school staff fall about half as fast as enrollment, as they did from FY2019 to FY2025, so
staffing that holds steady while enrollment falls would show up here first.

## The other readings

| Reading | When | What it tells you | The decision it informs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Births ({{fact:ind_births}} in {{fact:ind_births_year}}) and kindergartners per birth | Births each spring; kindergarten each October | Whether enrollment keeps falling or rebounds | School staffing; how much housing the schools can absorb |
| Open seats by school and grade | Each fall | How much room the schools have for children from new homes | Zoning pace; school staffing |
| New growth, and homes permitted | Each fall, when the tax rate is set | Whether revenue is keeping its pace, and how close the town is to a fire company | Zoning and permitting |
| Fire and medical calls, and response times | Each Financial Plan | Whether emergency demand keeps rising faster than housing | Fire and emergency medical staffing |
| Pay contracts and health premium renewals | As contracts settle; each spring | How much of the room the largest levers are using | Bargaining; plan design; whether an override is coming |

Births say the most, and earliest: they set kindergarten five years after birth, which is three to four years
after the state reports them, and that class's size through grade 12.

## When to revisit

- **Each budget cycle**, when the Town updates its long-range plan.
- **When the teachers' contracts settle.** Pay is the largest single number in this forecast.
- **Each school budget**, to see whether staff are falling with enrollment as the base case assumes.
- **When the district updates its enrollment forecast.**
- **Before the FY2031 budget**, when the pension money is freed and its use is decided.
