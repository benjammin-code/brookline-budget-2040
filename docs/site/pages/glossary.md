## Terms

### Assessed value
What the Town's assessors say a property is worth, for tax purposes. Rentals are valued
mostly on the rent they earn, and owned homes on what comparable homes sell for.

### Base case
This site's main forecast. It starts from the budget Town Meeting adopted in May 2026 and
assumes costs and revenue keep behaving as they did from FY2011 to FY2025. Every other result
on these pages is a change from it.

### Breakeven
The point at which a new home's property tax just covers the cost of the Town services and
schooling it adds, usually stated as a number of school-age children per home.

### Chapter 70
The state's main aid program for public schools. It is the largest part of Brookline's
[[state aid]].

### Cropper-McKibben
The demographers who prepared the School Department's 2023 enrollment forecast. Their forecast
has enrollment rising again to about 7,400 by FY2034. See [the three forecasts compared](@topic-enrollment).

### Debt exclusion
A vote to raise property taxes above the [[levy limit]] for one specific borrowing, such as a
school building, until that debt is paid off. It runs through the [[General Fund]] but is
matched dollar for dollar against the debt and pays for nothing else, so the forecast sets it
aside on both the revenue and the spending side; the spending history and the tax bill page
include it.

### Enterprise fund
A separate fund for a service that pays for itself through charges, such as water and sewer or
the golf course, kept apart from the [[General Fund]].

### Fiscal year
Brookline's budget year runs from July 1 to June 30 and is named for the year it ends: FY2027
runs from July 2026 to June 2027.

### Free cash
Money left over at the end of a fiscal year, mostly because revenue came in above budget and
some appropriations went unspent, once the state certifies it. The Town spends it two budgets
later, mostly on capital.

### FTE
Full-time equivalent: a way of counting staff in which two half-time positions count as one.

### General Fund
The Town's main operating fund: schools, Town departments, benefits, debt and capital paid for
from taxes and general revenue. About 90% of the Town's money runs through it.

### Levy limit
The most Brookline may collect in property tax in a year under [[Proposition 2½]]: last year's
limit plus 2.5%, plus [[new growth]], plus any [[override]] voters approve.

### Local receipts
Revenue other than property tax and state aid: motor vehicle excise, meals and hotel taxes,
fees, permits, fines and investment income.

### NESDEC
The New England School Development Council, whose enrollment forecast the [[base case]] uses.
Adjusted for the births since it was made, it projects enrollment falling to
{{fact:enroll_fy2040_about}} by FY2040. See [the three forecasts compared](@topic-enrollment).

### New growth
Tax on value added to the town by construction, renovation and new business equipment. It is
added to the [[levy limit]] on top of the 2.5%, permanently, with no vote.

### OPEB
Other post-employment benefits, mainly retiree health insurance. The unfunded OPEB liability is
what the Town has promised in future retiree health costs beyond what it has set aside.

### Override
A vote to raise the [[levy limit]] permanently. Brookline has passed five since 2005, most
recently in May 2026.

### Pension liability
What the Town owes in future pensions beyond what its retirement fund holds. The Town pays it
down on a schedule set to reach full funding in FY2030.

### Proposition 2½
The 1980 state law that caps how fast a town's total property tax can grow: 2.5% a year plus
[[new growth]], unless voters approve an [[override]] or a [[debt exclusion]].

### Residential exemption
A reduction in the taxable value of an owner-occupied home, $354,974 in FY2026. It does not
change how much the Town collects; it shifts tax from owner-occupied homes to rentals and
second homes.

### Reserves
Money the Town holds for emergencies and to smooth bad years, including stabilization funds and
unspent fund balance. Town policy keeps them at or above 20% of revenue.

### Room
Revenue minus the cost of continuing today's services. Positive room can pay for new priorities
or capital; negative room means cuts or new revenue.

### State aid
Money from the Commonwealth, mainly [[Chapter 70]] school aid and unrestricted local aid, net of
state and MBTA assessments.

### Students per birth
Public school enrollment divided by the children born to Brookline residents in the 13 years
that feed kindergarten through grade 12. It rises when families with children move in or stay.
