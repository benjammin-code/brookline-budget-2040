# Expense Drivers, Phase A: what actually drove historical growth

Companion to `expense_drivers_decomposition.csv` and `expense_drivers_fte_panel_fy04_27.csv`. Plan: `docs/expense_drivers_workplan.md`. Generated by `scripts/expense_drivers_phase_a.py`.

## The question

For each expense bucket, was growth more people, more expensive people, or something that is not people at all? No external data, no forecasting, no causal claim.

## The identity

```
    Spend = FTE x CostPerFTE
    (1 + g_spend) = (1 + g_FTE) x (1 + g_cost)
```

`g_cost` is computed as the exact residual of that identity rather than measured separately, so the two terms always recompose to observed growth. That is an accounting identity, not a finding, and the script verifies it to machine precision as a control.

## What the measure is, and is not

`spend_per_fte` is **Schedule A category expenditure divided by budgeted funded FTE**. It is not salary per FTE. Schedule A is actual expenditure and carries everything non-personnel: contracts, materials, fleet, utilities, solid-waste hauling, library acquisitions. FTE counts budgeted funded positions, not filled ones. Section A3 quantifies how much that matters per bucket.

## Two seams, flagged rather than smoothed

**FY2020/FY2021.** The FTE panel joins two sources that share no year: `department_fte_category_fy04_20.csv` and `department_fte_annual_fy21_27.csv`. The join therefore **cannot be cross-validated**, unlike the FY2021-2027 stitch itself, which was checked across three Financial Plans with zero discrepancies. Headcount rises +18.51 FTE across that seam after sixteen years of flatness, and this analysis cannot tell you whether that is hiring or a change of basis. Every decomposition is reported pre-seam, post-seam and full-window so the reader can see what depends on it.

**FY2025.** `town_fte_general_fund_fy04_27.csv` matches the category sums exactly through FY2024 and then diverges by 39-44 FTE, because those years use a different measure (Net General Funded Positions). This analysis uses the category/annual series throughout so the basis stays consistent.

## What Phase A does not do

- **No causal claim.** These are accounting decompositions. That a bucket grew through cost per head says nothing about why, and the residual has not been separated into wage growth, step progression, turnover or role mix. That is Phase B, and it needs an external wage index.
- **No link to growth drivers.** Whether headcount responds to new housing or new commercial space is Phase C, deliberately untouched here.
- **No non-personnel model.** A3 measures how large the non-personnel share is; it does not model what drives it.
- **No forecasting.** Phase D.
