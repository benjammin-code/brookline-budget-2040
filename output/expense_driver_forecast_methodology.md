# Driver-based expense forecast, FY2026-2040: methodology and first results

Companion to `expense_driver_forecast_fy26_40.csv` (base case by line),
`expense_driver_forecast_sensitivity_fy40.csv` (one dial at a time),
`expense_driver_forecast_growth_types.csv` (revenue and cost by type of growth)
and `expense_driver_forecast_reconciliation.csv`. Generated by
`scripts/expense_driver_forecast.py`, which prints its controls. Plan and Ben's
decisions: `docs/forecast_phase_d_plan.md`. Status: **base case set by Ben
2026-09-28 to Brookline's FY2011-25 record** (a base showing large surpluses
would not be credible). Page: (an earlier draft, not published)
("What Decides Brookline's 2040 Budget", source `docs/forecast_2040_page.html`).

## Basis

Annual General Fund **operating** spending: the Town's budget basis, excluding
excluded debt, which is matched dollar for dollar by the debt-exclusion tax.
This matches the revenue side (`base_case_decomposition.py`, `as_legislated`,
which includes the May 2026 override).

Levels start from the Town's FY2026 budget. Growth from FY2027 comes from the
drivers. Pensions, retiree health (OPEB), other benefits and within-levy debt
use the Town's published FY2026-31 schedules. CIP, reserves and non-appropriated
lines use the existing forecast's series, since they are policy lines.

## How each line is built

| Line | Formula | Source of each input |
|---|---|---|
| Town departments | FY2026 budget x [77% personnel x pay index x FTE index + 23% non-personnel x CPI] + growth costs | Personnel share from FY2019 salary shares on FY2025 spending; FTE at the FY2027 budgeted level, then flat |
| Schools | FY2026 budget x [83% personnel x pay index x staff index + 17% non-personnel x CPI x enrollment index], with 67% of each part varying with enrollment and 33% fixed | 83% derived in the driver tree; 67% from RKG's incremental school budget (set with Town staff) |
| Enrollment | NESDEC October 2023 projection, scaled up 1.0% to the October 2025 actual (6,949 vs 6,880), extended to FY2040 with NESDEC's own grade-to-grade ratios and kindergarten held at 423 | `data/NESDEC-Enrollment-Forecast.pdf` |
| School staff | Enrollment / 5.62 for the variable share | FY2025 ratio |
| Group health | FY2026 budget x subscribers x premiums; actives (51%) follow total FTE, retirees (49%) grow 0.74%/yr as in FY2011-25 | FY2027 Financial Plan subscriber table |
| Pensions | Town schedule to FY2031 (full funding FY2030), then normal cost growing with town payroll | FY27-31 LRFP |
| OPEB | Town schedule to FY2031, including the shift of pension money in FY2031 ($43.0M), flat after | FY27-31 LRFP |
| Other benefits | Town schedule to FY2031, then total payroll | FY27-31 LRFP |
| Within-levy debt | Town capital funding table to FY2032, then 3.5%/yr | FY2027 Financial Plan p.384 (FY28-32); capital policy ties capital to revenue |
| Growth | See below | |

## Base-case assumptions: Brookline's FY2011-25 record

Pay 3.0%/yr (Town target), with school pay +0.4 pt and Public Safety +1.5 pts
above it (their cost per head ran above the national benchmark). School staff
kept at today's level as enrollment falls (as after FY2019), with students from
above-trend growth still staffed at 5.62. NESDEC enrollment. Health premiums
12%/yr FY2027-29, then 8%. CPI 2.5%. Town cost of a new home $660 (FY2025 $).
OPEB follows the Town's schedule. Growth at `trend_continues`.

Ben's first settings (ratio held so staff follow enrollment, pay exactly at
target, premiums 6.5%) gave +$62.7M a year ex-override by FY2040; they are now
levers in the sensitivity table rather than the base.

## Growth by type

| Type | Revenue | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| New residential units | New growth tax, then +2.5%/yr under the levy limit | $660/home town services + 23.6% benefits loading (RKG's own ratio) + 0.2442 students/home at the variable school cost per student plus their staff's health insurance |
| Residential renovation | New growth tax | none modeled |
| New commercial space | New growth tax | 1 employee per 200 sqft x RKG $219 x department share, + benefits loading |
| Commercial renovation, personal property | New growth tax | none |
| Chestnut Hill (CHCA) | Project property tax and local receipts, ramped FY2029-34 | 266 homes at 0.126 students (high-rise), ~790 employees from the City Realty scenario averages (65,000 sqft medical office, 84,000 sqft retail, 192-room hotel) |

NESDEC's projection already contains students from trend-level housing, so the
forecast itself adds only students from homes above the trend scenario (and from
CHCA). The growth-type table counts every new home's students, because it asks
what each type costs in full.

## Controls

1. FY2026 before growth equals the Town's FY2026 operating budget ($391.0M).
2. Lines sum to the total every year.
3. Moving the health premium moves only group health; moving cost per home moves
   only the growth lines. (This control failed once when the base premium moved to
   8% and the test still used 8%; it now tests base + 2 pts.)
4. NESDEC's grade table sums to its own PK-12 totals in every year.
5. Backcast: the method run over FY2011-25 with actual drivers and no leftover
   predicts town departments 3.8% high and schools 5.7% low.

Bug caught on the switch to this base: "keep staff" first froze staff even for
students from above-trend growth, which made residential-led growth look +$51M
better than trend. Fixed: kept staff plus staff for above-trend students.

New homes' tax grows only 2.5% a year under the levy limit, while the cost of
their students and services grows with pay and prices, so a new home's net
position worsens over time. By FY2040 new residential units run a small net
cost at the model's student yield; renovations and commercial growth are net
revenue. Renovation is modeled with no cost, which overstates its net if
renovations add bedrooms and students.

## Limits

- Pay growth is one rate for all staff in the base case; contracts differ.
- The school variable share (67%) is RKG's judgment with Town staff, not a
  measured response.
- NESDEC's projection is from October 2023 and should be refreshed when a newer
  one is published.
- OPEB is held flat in nominal dollars after FY2031, as in the Town's schedule;
  there is no published schedule beyond it.
- Revenue is the existing forecast's; nothing on the revenue side was changed.

## Cost of one added student (added 2026-09-29)

Computed and checked by `marginal_student_cost()` in `scripts/expense_driver_forecast.py`;
written to `output/marginal_cost_per_student.csv` on every run.

**Method.** The school line treats 67% of the budget as varying with enrollment
(`SCHOOL_VARIABLE_SHARE`, RKG's incremental school budget set with Town staff) and 33%
as fixed. The variable share of staff is held at 5.62 students per staff member
(`RATIO_NOW`, FY2025). So one added student brings:

| Component | Formula | FY2026 $ |
|---|---|---:|
| Positions | 0.67 / 5.62 = 0.119 (one per 8.4 students) | |
| Staff | 0.119 x $95,851 (FY2026 school budget x 83% personnel share / 1,236.5 staff) | $11,427 |
| Non-personnel | FY2026 school budget x 17% / 6,949 students ($3,493) x 0.67 | $2,340 |
| Health insurance | 0.119 x $10,866 (FY2026 group health x 51% active share / 1,954 total FTE) | $1,295 |
| Other payroll-linked benefits | Medicare etc., from the model | $206 |
| **Total** | | **$15,269** |

Average school budget per pupil in FY2026 is $20,549, so the marginal figure is 74% of
the average. Nominal cost in the model: $20,726 (FY2034), $26,120 (FY2040). RKG's
$15,572 (FY2025) uses the same 67% and lands close by construction. Teacher pensions are
paid by the state (MTRS) and are excluded; the pension line scales only with town
payroll.

**Control.** The script adds 100 students (via CHCA's student count, fully ramped by
FY2034), differences the full model, deflates the school line by the model's own pay
and price indices, and requires it to equal staff + non-personnel above. PASS at $13,768.
The first version of this check multiplied by the personnel share twice and failed; the
failure was in the check, not the model.

**What it doesn't capture.** Real marginal cost is lumpy. K-8 classes average 17-21
against guidance of 22-25 (Superintendent Report 9/16/26), so a student joining a grade
with room costs little and one that forces a new section costs a teacher. The figure is
a long-run average of that, and the 67% split is a judgment, not a measured response.

## Chestnut Hill (CHCA) (added 2026-09-29)

**Additive to trend growth**, on revenue (levy grows at the trend rate, CHCA's
property-tax increments added on top and then compounding in the levy; rooms tax and
excise added to local receipts and run through the free-cash cycle) and on cost (266
homes at $660, 0.126 students per home, ~963 employees).

**Revenue level: the approved program.** The five published scenarios are different
building programs (City Realty 1/2/3: 668/424/388 homes, 184k/213k/51k sqft office and
retail, 200/225/152 rooms; RKG: 265 homes, 74k sqft, 100 rooms), which is why revenue
ranges $3.2M-6.7M. The approved plan (266 homes, 200 rooms, >50% commercial floor area)
is priced as RKG's residential tax per home x 266 ($1,848,036) + City Realty 1's
commercial and personal property tax ($2,696,433; the only scenario with 200 rooms, and
its 184k sqft is consistent with the >50% commercial rule) = **$4,544,469**, plus rooms
tax for 200 rooms ($762,120) and RKG's excise per home x 266 ($99,750) =
**$861,870** in local receipts. Commercial square footage is the unknown.

The committed Phase 2 file keeps City Realty's average; the driver forecast reruns the
growth-scenario model into a scratch file for each CHCA setting
(`chca_growth_frame()`), so nothing committed changes.

**Levers (FY2040 balance; FY2026-40 total):** City Realty average +$1.3M / +$11.1M; RKG
&minus;$2.4M / &minus;$20.6M; counted inside trend growth (no separate revenue or cost)
&minus;$5.9M / &minus;$51.3M. Base-case balance without the override is now &minus;$2.6M
(FY2031) and &minus;$13.2M (FY2040).

## Re-anchored to the adopted FY2027 budget (2026-09-29)

**Supersedes the FY2026-anchored results above.** The forecast now takes its FY2027
levels from the budget adopted by the May 26, 2026 Annual Town Meeting (Article 8,
"FY2027 BUDGET - TABLE 1", FY27 FINAL column; `data/Combined-Reports-May-2026-ATM.pdf`;
constants in `scripts/fy2027_adopted.py`), and applies drivers from FY2028. FY2026 is
still the Town's FY2026 budget, for the balanced-budget check.

Why: FY2027 is under way, and the February FY2027 Financial Plan was a no-override
budget (Town departments $101.26M, schools $144.53M). The adopted budget, with the
override, is Town departments $103,486,124, School Department $149,801,900 (22.1 fewer
FTE than FY2026), employee benefits $99,123,428 (pensions $41.17M, group health
$46.36M, OPEB $4.93M), borrowing $49,425,313 (of which $35,379,833 excluded), general
$15,614,820 (including a new $3,568,303 Override Stabilization Fund), revenue-financed
capital $22,409,889, non-appropriated $10,792,034; total $450,668,508. Revenue: free
cash $18,280,805, other available funds $8,755,561 and budgeted local receipts
$37,633,556 are fixed by the adopted budget and now used in both revenue scripts.

Changes in the method: school staff start at FY2026 minus 22.1 (about 1,214; FY2027
ratio 5.67 students per staff); growth costs count only units added from FY2028; health
premiums +12% in FY2028-29 (FY2027's increase is in the level), then 8%.

Controls: FY2027 equals the adopted operating budget ($415.29M) to the dollar; FY2026
and FY2027 revenue minus spending come out +$1.3M and +$2.7M (both adopted balanced).

Results: with the override (the forecast) +$9.1M FY2028, +$20.1M FY2031, +$23.4M FY2035,
+$19.4M FY2040. Without the override's revenue (keeping the spending it funded) about
&minus;$7M a year to the mid-2030s, &minus;$18.8M FY2040. Cost per added student $16,236
(FY2027 dollars). Town plan implied: departments ~1.5%/yr pay; schools ~6.6%/yr pay or
~1,350 FTE by FY2031. FY2031 bridge from the Town's &minus;$42.7M: +27.3 override,
+10.7 levy, +8.2 aid/receipts/free cash, +15.8 schools, &minus;5.4 departments, +4.8
capital/other, +1.5 benefits/rounding = +$20.1M.

Open: the $3.57M Override Stabilization Fund deposit is treated as one-time; if the Town
draws it down in FY2028-29, revenue in those years is understated.
