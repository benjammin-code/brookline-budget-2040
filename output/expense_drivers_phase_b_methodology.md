# Expense Drivers, Phase B: how much of it was just price?

Companion to `expense_drivers_phase_b_attribution.csv`. Plan: `docs/expense_drivers_workplan.md`. Generated by `scripts/expense_drivers_phase_b.py`. Phase A is the prerequisite and its methodology doc explains the FY2010 reclassification and the FY2020/21 FTE seam that set these windows.

## The counterfactual

For each town category, the spend Brookline would have had if headcount moved exactly as observed, the personnel part grew with the national ECI for state and local government workers, and the non-personnel part grew with Boston CPI without scaling to headcount:

```
    predicted(y1) = personnel(y0) x (FTE1/FTE0) x (ECI1/ECI0)
                  + nonpersonnel(y0) x (CPI1/CPI0)
```

The gap is the **residual**. It is a residual and nothing more: it contains step and grade progression, turnover savings, role mix, service level changes, and any local deviation from national pay trends, with no way here to separate them.

## Limitations

- **The ECI is national.** Not published for metro areas at this industry detail. If Massachusetts public-sector pay outran the national average, every residual here is overstated by that difference.
- **The personnel share is one year.** FY2019, held fixed across each window. Drift in that share is absorbed by the residual.
- **Fiscal against calendar years.** Each fiscal year is matched to the mean of the two calendar years it straddles.
- **`spend_per_fte` is not salary per FTE**, carried over from Phase A. This is why the non-personnel term exists at all, and why the residual is least trustworthy for the two categories with the largest non-personnel shares, Public Works and Culture & Recreation.
