# What new homes do to the budget, year by year: method

Companion to `growth_links_revenue.csv`, `growth_links_services.csv`,
`growth_links_enrollment.csv`, `growth_links_children_by_town.csv`, `growth_links_sales.csv`
and `growth_links.json` (the site's chart data). Generated by `scripts/growth_links.py`
(2026-10-11) for the topic page `docs/site/pages/topic-growth-links.md`.

## The question
A project-level fiscal estimate assumes the budget moves home by home. Each test below checks
one link against what happened, and says how far ahead each reading can be seen.

## Results and how far to trust them

| Test | Result | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Permits to new growth | One year on, 0.69 to 0.98 of permitted cost becomes certified new growth valuation (median 0.78), FY2018-FY2025 permits | Level steady; 8 years; correlation weak (r = 0.37) because both series move little |
| Homes and Town staff | Homes +8% FY2011-25; police and fire positions 339 to 334; other departments flat to FY2020, then +40 to FY2025 | Descriptive; homes line interpolated between censuses |
| Brookline enrollment | K-12 5,883 (fall 2006) to 7,506 (fall 2019) to 6,701 (fall 2025); kindergarten 0.76-1.01 times births five years earlier | Descriptive, 20 years |
| Children per added home | 0.28 (0.15 to 0.41) against 0.55 per existing home; 252 towns, 2010-2020; growth-rate form; 0.30 without Boston, weighted by homes | Cross-sectional, one decade; the level form (change in children on change in homes) gives 0.14 without Boston and is driven by large cities, so the rate form is used |
| Home sales | Single-family sales vs kindergartners per birth, same year: r = 0.48 (n = 16, fall 2020 left out); one year on 0.39; two years 0.07; condos with 3+ bedrooms 0.03 | Suggestive only |

## Superseded claim (withdrawn 2026-10-11, see below)
The 2026-09-15 note's "correlation peaks at lag 1, r = 0.65, n = 8" for permits against new
growth does not reproduce with the permit file deduplicated on RECORD ID and DLS total new growth
valuation (r = 0.37 at one year, -0.36 at two). Its two-year ratios (0.75, 1.10, 0.69, 0.59,
0.95, 0.90) do reproduce. The page uses the one-year ratio range, the tightest.

## Data limits
- The Census Bureau permit survey counts units in new buildings only (about 32 a year
  2006-2019 for Brookline); the Census count of homes grew about 150 a year 2010-2020, so the
  survey is used only for the timing of new construction.
- Sale records come from each roll's "most recent sale" field; two sales between rolls count
  once. FY2022's sheet in the consolidated workbook has no header row and is skipped. Bedrooms
  are missing for single-family homes and about a quarter of condominiums, so "family-sized"
  could not be defined for single-family sales; single-family sales are used whole.
- Children are residents under 18, not public school students.
- Cached sales: `data/permits_cache/assessment_roll_sales.csv` (delete to rebuild from rolls,
  about 40 seconds).

## Update 2026-10-11: revenue timing
The revenue test now lines up permits by calendar year against new growth three fiscal years
later, following the January 1 assessment date: r = 0.67, ratios 0.74 to 0.98 (n = 6), the best
of the 12-, 24- and 36-month windows tested. That reproduces the 2026-09-15 r of about 0.65, so
the "superseded claim" above is withdrawn: the earlier note was right, and the fiscal-year
alignment used in the first version of this page was the weaker one.
