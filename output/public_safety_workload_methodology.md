# Police and fire workload, FY2003-2025

Companion to `public_safety_workload_fy03_25.csv`. Generated by
`scripts/public_safety_workload.py`. Values transcribed from the "Performance /
Workload Indicators" tables in sixteen Town Financial Plans (FY2006-FY2027; the
FY2014, FY2016, FY2017, FY2019, FY2023 and FY2024 plans were downloaded
2026-09-29 from Budget Central). Actual columns only.

## Data quality

- Two plans repeat one year's values under another year's label. Fire figures of
  7,567 responses and 4,132 medical calls appear as FY2010 in the FY2012 plan and
  as FY2012 in the FY2014 and FY2015 plans. A police total of 75,525 appears as
  both FY2014 and FY2015. Those years are flagged and left out of growth rates.
- FY2019-23 gaps filled 2026-10-10 from the FY2021-FY2025 budgets, which exist
  only as OpenGov "stories" with the workload tables as images (copies in
  `data/fire_reports/opengov_images/`; every value checked against them). Reported
  fire totals are used for every year; components come to 97-99% of them.
- FY2019 police self-initiated calls (26,657) equal FY2018's, probably a carried-over
  value. FY2023 police figures are round (50,000 and 26,000) though labelled actual.
  The FY2025 OpenGov fire table shows FY2022 as 7,862, FY2021's total repeated; the
  FY2024 table's 9,089, consistent with its own components, is used.
- The fill keeps the shape of both series: fire responses 8,298 (FY2018), 8,523,
  7,980, 7,862 (the COVID dip), 9,089, 9,358, 9,976, 10,491 (FY2025); police calls
  67,117, 68,505, 59,611, 52,978, 56,167, about 50,000, 52,529, 55,619.
- Police "calls" are dispatch counts. The drop after FY2018 may partly reflect
  changes in how calls are recorded (self-initiated calls are reported
  separately from FY2014); the plans don't say.

## Findings

| Series | Window | Growth/yr | Housing units/yr |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Fire total responses | FY2003-18 | +1.6% | +0.6% |
| Fire total responses | FY2018-25 | +3.4% | +0.5% |
| Medical emergencies | FY2013-25 | +2.7% | +0.6% |
| Medical emergencies | FY2021-25 | +7.4% | +0.5% |
| Police-related calls | FY2003-13 | +2.9% | +0.6% |
| Police-related calls | FY2016-24 | &minus;4.0% | +0.5% |

**Police workload is falling while housing grows.** Dispatch calls peaked around
75,600 in FY2013 and were 52,500-55,600 in FY2024-25. Whatever the reason, there
is no sign of housing growth adding police workload, which supports a low
marginal police cost for new homes.

**Fire and medical workload is rising, much faster than housing.** Total fire
responses grew from about 7,300 (FY2013) to 10,500 (FY2025), 3.0% a year, about
five times the rate of housing growth. Medical emergencies drive much of it. At
FY2025's rate of about 0.37 responses per housing unit, the ~150 homes added a
year account for about 55 more responses a year, against an observed increase of
several hundred a year. Something other than new housing is driving it; age
structure is the obvious candidate to test.

**Fire staffing didn't rise with workload.** Public Safety positions fell by 5
over FY2011-25. The Town's own account of the Fire Department's recurring
overtime deficit (FY2027 Financial Plan) attributes it to leave rules changed in
the FY2018 contract, injury leave up about 150% from FY2018 to FY2025, and a 2022
arbitration award on parental leave, and describes a proposed "flexible staffing
model" allowing three-firefighter companies.
