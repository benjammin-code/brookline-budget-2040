# Police and fire staffing: Brookline against peers, and how staffing scales

Generated by `scripts/public_safety_staffing_peers.py` (2026-10-04). Question from Ben:
do we have enough public-safety resources, and will they grow if the population grows?

## Sources
- Police: FBI, *Crime in the United States 2019*, Table 78, Massachusetts (full-time
  officers by city). 2019 is the last complete year before the FBI's switch to
  incident-based reporting left gaps in Massachusetts coverage, and before 2020-22
  attrition. Brookline's 128 officers matches the Town's own count (130 sworn, Nov 2018,
  Advisory Committee FY23 report). Belmont did not report in 2019.
- Fire: U.S. Fire Administration, National Fire Department Registry, Massachusetts
  download (career, volunteer, paid-per-call firefighters; stations). Self-reported and
  not dated per department. Brookline's 161 matches its budgeted Fire FTE (161, FY2021-26).
- Population, housing units, land area: 2020 Census redistricting files.
- Brookline staffing history: Town Financial Plans (department FTE, FY2004-2027).

## Results
Officers per 1,000 residents (2019 officers / 2020 population): Brookline 2.03; Cambridge
2.26, Watertown 1.98, Medford 1.71, Newton 1.68, Somerville 1.57, Needham 1.53, Wellesley
1.39, Arlington 1.38, Lexington 1.34; Boston 3.17. At 2022's 116 sworn, Brookline would be
1.84 (peers' 2022 figures not yet pulled).

Career firefighters per 1,000: Brookline 2.55 (5 stations); Watertown 2.49, Cambridge 2.35,
Boston 2.22, Newton 2.07, Needham 2.03, Medford 2.03, Belmont 1.98, Somerville 1.88,
Wellesley 1.86, Arlington 1.68, Lexington 1.68.

Across Massachusetts towns of 10,000+ (log staff on log population and log density):
police elasticity to population 1.06 (n=162, R2 0.94), density adds nothing; career
firefighters in mostly-career departments 0.94 (n=105, R2 0.89). Predicted for Brookline:
121 officers (actual 128, +6%), 124 career firefighters (actual 161, +30%).

## Reading
- Levels: across towns, bigger towns carry proportionally more police and firefighters.
  Brookline is at or above what its size and density predict, slightly for police and
  well above for fire. Its police shortfall is against its own authorized strength
  (135-139), not against peers.
- Changes: within Brookline, population rose 7.6% from 2010 to 2020 while budgeted
  police and fire staffing stayed flat (fire 168 in FY2004, 161 since FY2010). Fire
  staffing is set by stations and companies, each staffed around the clock, so it moves
  in steps when coverage requires, not per resident. This is why the forecast's marginal
  public-safety cost per home (~$230) is far below an average-cost allocation (RKG ~$980).
- A sustained, much larger increase in population would, on the cross-town evidence,
  eventually bring roughly proportional staffing; Brookline's ~0.6% a year of housing
  growth does not trigger it within a decade.

## Limits
- Per-resident ratios ignore daytime population (commuters, Longwood medical area,
  visitors), which raises Brookline's need relative to residents.
- 2019 police counts predate the 2020-22 attrition; registry fire counts are undated.
- Cross-sectional elasticities describe long-run levels across towns, not how one
  town's staffing responds year to year.
