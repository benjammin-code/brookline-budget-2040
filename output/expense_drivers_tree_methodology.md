# Expense driver tree, FY2011-2025

Companion to `expense_drivers_tree_fy11_25.csv` and `expense_drivers_object_class_fy11_24.csv`. Generated by `scripts/expense_drivers_tree.py`, which prints its own controls. Builds on Phases A-C (`docs/expense_drivers_workplan.md`).

General Fund expenditure (Schedule A) went from $187.2M in FY2011 to $364.7M in FY2025, $177.4M of growth. Every dollar of that is assigned to one of nine driver groups below, and the script checks that they sum back to the total exactly.

## Commercial space

Non-residential new growth value FY2012-25 was $688.4M, but that includes personal property (mostly utility equipment), which creates no service demand. In FY2026, the one year with a published split, personal property was 74% of non-residential new growth. At that split, about 348,976 sqft of commercial space was added, roughly 7% of today's ~5.1M sqft stock, costing about $0.4M/yr at RKG's $219 per employee. Treating all non-residential growth as commercial gives an upper bound of $1.5M. Either way it is small beside new homes ($1.7M) and far below the other drivers.

Non-personnel spending grew at or below local inflation, depending on the end year; FY2023 to FY2024 alone moved it 15%, mostly services. The 'Other' line grew from $0.8M to $2.8-4.9M and looks like a change in what the Town books there, so it is shown both ways. Either way, non-personnel is not a source of above-inflation growth, which is what the tree's CPI assumption for it takes for granted.

Only three years are used. The Financial Plans' multi-year class tables for FY2014-FY2018 repeat values across differently labelled columns (the FY2020 plan's FY2016 column is the FY2018 plan's FY2015 column, for example), so those years were not trusted.

## Estimates and judgment calls, in order of how much they matter

1. **Pension normal cost in FY2011 is carried back two years** ($3.4M) from the 1/1/2014 valuation's FY2013 employer normal cost ($3.43M, 5.48% of payroll) by wage inflation and town headcount. The first version of this script estimated $4.87M by deflating FY2025, which missed the rise in normal cost as a share of payroll (5.48% to 7.71%). That rise is now its own component under Pay & costs above inflation. The 1/1/2010 valuation would pin FY2011 exactly; the remaining error is a few hundred thousand dollars.
2. **Growth-driven town staffing uses RKG's rates, department share only.** RKG's $1,371/home includes $262 of benefits, debt and insurance; only the 81% that is department budgets is used here, because benefits for added staff are counted under Benefits. The first version used the full $1,371 and so counted those benefits twice. RKG puts 88% of the department share in police and fire; Brookline's actual added town positions FY2011-25 were in other departments (public safety FTE fell by 5). It is an allocation, not an observation.
3. **Students from new homes** use the model's 0.2442 per home, giving 305, which happens to equal net enrollment change (6,627 to 7,023). That coincidence hides the real path: enrollment rose to 7,855 by FY2019, far beyond what new homes could supply, then fell with COVID. School staff rose with it and fell only part way. Of the staff added beyond enrollment, about 151 FTE came while enrollment was rising (FY2011-19) and about 73 FTE is staff retained after it fell (FY2019-25), measured separately in each sub-window. A Town/Schools count of 10 developments of 8+ units built since 2015 (ERSC report, PDF p.567) found 0.03-0.56 enrolled students per unit in Fall 2025, simple average about 0.21.
4. **School personnel share (81%) is derived**, not published: FY2011 General Fund personnel actuals minus town-department personnel, over school spending. FY2024 on the same basis gives about 83%. Added school staff are priced at $93,800 (FY2025 pay, salary only). The first version priced them at total school spending per staff member (~$116K), which included non-personnel costs.
5. **Town FTE crosses the unvalidated FY2020/21 seam** (+18.5 FTE), which sits inside 'Staffing levels'.
6. **Group health residual includes retirees**, who are about half of plan subscribers (1,659 of 3,390 in FY2025) and do not scale with active headcount.
7. **Commercial split uses one year** (FY2026) of the commercial/personal property mix.
8. **The pay benchmark is national.** BLS does not publish the ECI by state. QCEW average pay for local government grew 3.40%/yr in Massachusetts and 3.65%/yr nationally over 2014-2024 (`qcew_local_gov_pay_ma_us_2014_24.csv`), so the national benchmark does not understate Massachusetts pay growth.

## What this is not

Not causal, and not a forecast. It is an accounting of where the money went, with each piece measured against an outside benchmark where one exists. Phase D would turn it into a forecast.
